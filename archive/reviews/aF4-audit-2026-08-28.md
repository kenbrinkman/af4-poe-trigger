# aF4 Master Reference — Adversarial Audit, 2026-08-28

Independent audit of `aF4-MASTER-REFERENCE.md` (rev D) against primary sources: the
Olimex ESP32-PoE-ISO Rev N KiCad files (cloned fresh from the OLIMEX GitHub repo),
manufacturer datasheets and live distributor listings, inD aquatics' published 0-10 V
setup guide, and the repo's own source files (`pcb/gen_pcb.py`, the generated `.kicad_pcb`,
the drill file, `pcb/make_package.py`, `firmware/af4-feeder.yaml`, BOM/centroid CSVs). All §2
arithmetic was re-derived numerically. Provenance tags were not trusted.

**Verdict in one line:** the electrical design and the arithmetic hold up, the firmware
matches the document, and 19 of 20 MPNs are real, active, and stocked — but the board
as generated cannot be built, because **U1's footprint does not match the AQY212GS
package**, and two further items tagged `[DS]`/implied-verified are wrong or stale.

---

## A. Build-blocking — fix before the PCBWay requote is answered

### A1. U1 footprint is the wrong package geometry (unbuildable as generated)

`pcb/gen_pcb.py` places U1 on KiCad's `Package_SO:SO-4_4.4x2.3mm_P1.27mm` — a generic
SO-4 whose library description traces to an **OPTEK OPIA403** optocoupler: 2.3 mm-deep
body, pads on a **1.27 mm pitch** (pad centres at y = ±0.635 mm), pad columns at
x = ±3.15 mm.

The Panasonic AQY212GS is not that package:

- Panasonic's GU SOP4 series page gives the body as **4.3 × 4.4 × 2.2 mm** — nearly
  square, not 4.4 × 2.3.
- JLCPCB's part listing for AQY212GS gives the package as **"SOP-4-2.54mm"** —
  **2.54 mm terminal pitch**, i.e. pins at ±1.27 mm, exactly twice the footprint's
  ±0.635 mm. Octopart's dimensional data (4.4 × 4.3 × 2.0) agrees with Panasonic.

Every pin misses its pad by ~0.6 mm along the pin row, and the body overhangs a
courtyard drawn for a package half its depth. PCBWay turn-key will either raise an
engineering query or produce five boards with an unmountable isolation relay. This
also invalidates the master doc's **"4.3 mm pad-to-pad across U1"** isolation figure
(§2 and §4) — that number describes the wrong footprint. The isolation *frontier*
(4.7 mm pour gap, GAP_L 121.65 → GAP_R 126.35) still exists, but the pad-to-pad
creepage across the correct part will be a different, smaller number and must be
re-derived. The correct land pattern should be taken from the dimension drawing in
Panasonic's GU SOP4 datasheet (`semi_eng_gu1a_aqy21_gs.pdf` — a binary PDF I could
not parse remotely; pull it and read the recommended mounting pad before
regenerating). Check afterwards that the 4.7 mm silk band still clears the new
courtyard, and that the re-derived creepage still supports the 1500 Vrms / SELV
argument in §4.

*How this got past DRC: same mechanism as the slot widths — DRC checks pads against
tracks, not pads against the physical part. The footprint was well-formed and wrong,
which is this project's signature failure mode (§7).*

### A2. D2's value in `pcb/gen_pcb.py` is `SMAJ15A`; everything else says `SMAJ13A`

```python
place("Diode_SMD", "D_SMA", "D2", "SMAJ15A", 128.20, 126.50, 270, ...)
```

The BOM CSV, `pcb/make_package.py`, and all prose say SMAJ13A. Turn-key assembly sources
from the BOM, so the boards would get the 13 V part — but the board file now
contradicts the BOM inside the "source of truth" script itself. If the 15 V standoff
was ever the *intent* (see D2 discussion in C4 below — a 15 V standoff would actually
retire the open-circuit-voltage worry), that decision was never propagated; if it
wasn't, the script is wrong. Either way one of them is lying. Fix the script.

## B. Findings that touch unattended operation on the tank

### B1. §2.4's load-bearing number is now verified — and the truth is *worse* than the doc says

Panasonic's published spec for AQY212GS (product page, checked 2026-08-28):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| LED operate current | **1.1 mA typ, 3 mA max** |
| LED turn-off current | **0.3 mA min**, 1.0 mA typ |
| Recommended LED operating range | 5–30 mA |

So the `[ASSERT]` ~1.1 mA typical was correct. But the figure that matters for the
GPIO13 argument is the one the doc never cites: there is **no minimum operate
current**, and the guaranteed-off threshold is **0.3 mA**. Re-solving the GPIO13 boot
node with a realistic sub-milliamp LED V_F (1.0–1.25 V rather than the doc's 1.25 V)
gives **0.72–0.85 mA** into the LED — higher than the doc's 0.68 mA, more than 2× the
guaranteed-off current, and inside the band where the datasheet simply does not
promise the relay stays open. "62 % of the way to a spurious feed" understated it:
on GPIO13 nothing guaranteed the feed *wouldn't* happen. The GPIO32 decision is not
just cheap insurance; it was mandatory. Open item 4 can be closed, and §2.4's framing
should be rewritten around the 0.3 mA turn-off spec.

(Verified clean on the Olimex side, from the Rev N KiCad source directly: R35 is
2.2 kΩ and hangs on GPIO13/I2C-SDA; GPIO32 appears on exactly two nets — the ESP32
module and EXT2 pin 6; EXT1 pin 3 is GND; EXT1/EXT2 pin 1 sit at (91.44, 123.22) and
(116.84, 123.22) on 2.54 mm pitch; board outline x 90.15–118.15, y 90.00–188.15 =
28.00 × 98.15 mm. Every `[CAD]` claim I could test is right.)

### B2. The 290 s lockout does not survive a reboot

`script.do_feed`'s state lives in RAM. Any reboot during the 300 s cycle — OTA
update, crash, brownout, power blip — clears the lockout silently: the switch comes
back `ALWAYS_OFF` (good, the pin is safe), but `binary_sensor.af4_lockout` reads
`off` and the device will accept a new press immediately. Sequence that ends with two
feeds ~2 minutes apart into the tank: scheduled feed fires → OTA or crash at t+60 s →
HA automation retry / second schedule slot / manual press → second feed. Low
probability, but it is exactly the class of unattended edge this document exists to
catch, and §5 doesn't mention it. Cheapest fix: an `on_boot` hook that runs the
lockout tail (or simply `script.execute` a 300 s lockout-only script) so every boot
starts locked out. That also covers the power-blip case for free.

### B3. The 300 s cycle boundary (§5, open item 6) — confirmed, widen it

Re-derived: 10 s ≥ 6 s (67 % margin), 290 s ≥ 60 s (383 % margin), 10+290 = 300 s
against "at least 5 minutes apart" — zero margin, and the inD guide's wording
("must be at least 5 minutes apart") gives no rounding grace. A 300 s tail costs
nothing. Do it together with B2's on_boot lockout and both edges disappear.

### B4. The web server is a second, unauthenticated control path

`web_server: port: 80` exposes the Feed button to anything on the LAN, with no
`auth:` block. The §5 safety story ("HA is scheduler only — it presses one button
and can do nothing else") is true of HA and false of the network. On a home LAN this
is a judgment call, not a defect — but the doc should either state the exposure or
the config should grow `web_server: auth: { username, password }` once commissioning
(step 6.5 uses this page) is done. Related hygiene: the API encryption key and OTA
password are committed in plaintext in the repo.

### B5. Held-high failure modes are bounded — worth stating as a positive result

Checked deliberately, since "unscheduled feed" is the nightmare: if the ESP32 crashes
with the SSR on, or the PhotoMOS fails shorted, the port sees a *continuously held*
10.4 V. Per the inD spec (verified, see D1) a held-high line produces **at most one
feed** and then never re-arms, because re-arming requires ~0 V for >60 s. Every
stuck-at fault therefore costs one feed, not a feeding loop. The doc never actually
walks through this; it should, because it is the single best safety property the
design has.

### C3. §2.2's headroom reasoning is wrong (the number was right, the comfort was not)

The doc: *"~1.18 V headroom against a dropout spec of 1.2 V at 800 mA. At 20 mA the
actual dropout is far smaller, so this passes."* The LM1117 is a quasi-LDO — NPN pass
device, dropout ≈ V_BE stack — and its dropout-vs-current curve is nearly **flat
around ~1 V**; it does not collapse at light load the way a PMOS LDO's does. Redo the
margin honestly (SS14 at ~0.3 V):

| Brick voltage | LDO input | Headroom to 10.47 V | Headroom to worst-case 10.88 V |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12.2 V | 11.90 | 1.43 V ✓ | 1.02 V — at the edge |
| 12.0 V | 11.70 | 1.23 V ✓ | 0.82 V — **in dropout** |
| 11.6 V | 11.30 | 0.83 V — **in dropout** | 0.42 V — deep in dropout |

Consequences: (a) a board whose divider stacks toward the high end rides dropout on a
nominal 12 V brick — output degrades gracefully to V_IN − ~1 V ≈ 10.3–10.7 V, still
inside the 9–11 window, so **this is not a safety problem**, but regulation and
ripple rejection are gone; (b) commissioning checks 6.2 and 6.3 are mutually
inconsistent at their edges — a brick legitimately passing 6.2 at 11.6 V cannot
deliver 10.3 V at TP2 even with perfect parts (~10.2–10.3 V max). If 6.3 reads
"low" at a low-but-passing 6.2, the divider may be innocent. Note it in the guide.

### C4. The 13 V TVS standoff question (§2.5, open item 5) — narrowed but still open

Measuring the brick's open-circuit voltage remains the right call. Context that makes
it less scary: a 12 V/12.5 A brick regulated to ±5 % idles ≤ 12.6 V, under D2's 13 V
standoff; and D2 sits *downstream of D1* on the board (see D2 mismatch below), so D2
sees brick-minus-a-Schottky. It would take a >13.3 V-idling brick to make D2 warm.
Measure once and close it — or note that the script's accidental "SMAJ15A" would
close it by fiat if 15 V standoff were adopted deliberately (breakdown 16.7–18.5 V
still clamps well under the AQY212's 60 V rating).

## D. Document vs sources — mismatches the tags didn't catch

### D1. The `[SPEC]` rows are verbatim-correct (verified against inD's published guide)

inD's 0-10 V setup guide (Neptune Systems page, fetched 2026-08-28): *"9v or higher
signal for greater than 6 seconds"*, *"approximately 0v … for greater than 60
seconds"*, *"Feed cycles must be at least 5 minutes apart."* R1–R3 exact. The product
page also confirms 12 V/12.5 A supply and the 3.5 mm 0-10 V interface (R5, R6). One
nuance: the guide states **no maximum voltage** — the "below 12 V" ceiling in §1 is
the designer's inference from the 10.37 V OEM measurement, not a published limit, and
the worst-case 10.88 V output already exceeds what the OEM dongle produces by 0.5 V.
Almost certainly fine for a threshold input, but that upper bound deserves its own
`[ASSERT]` tag; right now it reads as spec.

### D2. §2's circuit ASCII does not match the board it describes

The ASCII shows D2 clamping `+12V_RAW` (upstream, in parallel with F1's branch, with
D1 in a separate leg off the splitter). The generated board is a series chain:
J1 → F1 → D1 → `+12V` node, with **D2, C1 and the LDO all downstream of D1**. The
built topology is fine — arguably better (D2 never sees reverse input; F1 still
protects a shorted D2) — but an auditor working from §2 would sign off on a circuit
that was never built. Redraw the ASCII from `pcb/gen_pcb.py`'s netmap.

### D3. "44 joints" is a hard-coded fiction; the real count is 28

J1 = 3 terminals + 2 shield tabs, J2 = 3, J3 = 10, J4 = 10 → **28 joints**. The "4
THT parts / 44 joints" figure is hand-written in `pcb/make_package.py`'s README template
(where the surrounding numbers are computed), in `docs/aF4-pcb-notes.md`, the BOM
doc, and §4 of the master reference — and the README even itemises 5+3+10+10
directly above the wrong total. This is precisely the §7 failure mode ("a hard-coded
count in a generated document"), it survived the audit-oriented rewrite, and PCBWay
prices hand-soldered joints — you are asking to be quoted for 16 joints that don't
exist. Compute it from the board like the other numbers.

### D4. Minor errata

- §2.6 viewing angles: both LEDs are 120°, not 160°/140° (two distributor-datasheet
  sources). Doesn't change the resistor choices; does change the sight-hole optics
  story slightly.
- BOM CSV D3 description "2.0 V typ" vs the (correct) 2.2 V used in §2.2.
- §2.2 "divider 10.47/1008 = 10.39 mA": the load current actually drawn through R4
  is V_REF/R4 = 10.33 mA (10.02 mA at worst-case tolerance) — same conclusion vs the
  5 mA floor, slightly different physics; worth one sentence since this branch *is*
  the minimum-load guarantee.
- R5 dissipates ~96 mW continuously against a 125 mW 0805 rating (77 %). Fine at
  enclosure temperatures, but it is the hottest-running passive on the board and the
  doc doesn't mention it. A 0.25 W-rated 0805 (e.g. RC0805FR-7W…) is a drop-in if you
  ever want the margin; raising the divider impedance is *not* available, since the
  divider is the minimum-load ballast.
- §6 gives the hat mounting holes as (123.0, −118.0)/(123.5, −147.0), §4 as positive
  y — same holes, two coordinate conventions, never stated. The 0.5 mm x-asymmetry
  (123.0 vs 123.5) is real in `pcb/gen_pcb.py`, matches both docs, and appears deliberate.

## F. Still unverified after this pass

1. Supply open-circuit voltage vs 13 V standoff — needs a DMM (C4).
2. Feeder trigger-port input impedance/current draw — never measured; the whole load
   budget (F1 margin, LDO dissipation) silently assumes the port draws ~nothing.
   One measurement during an OEM-triggered feed closes it.
3. Feeder's internal 24 h timer behaviour under external triggering (§1) — bench test.
4. DRC and the enclosure's 13+3 checks — reported, not re-run here (no `pcbnew` in
   this environment); unchanged from the doc's own status.
5. Panasonic's recommended SOP4 land pattern — required input for the A1 fix; read it
   from the GU SOP4 datasheet PDF directly, don't trust a library by name. Ever.
6. LM1117 V_REF sub-bands (1.238–1.262 / 1.225–1.270): consistent with SNOS412 and
   with every secondary source checked (ESR window and 10 µF-tantalum requirement were
   independently confirmed), but I could not re-pull the exact table from TI's PDF in
   this environment. Confidence high, flagged for completeness.
